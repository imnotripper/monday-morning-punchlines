# Candidates — Monday Morning Punchlines

Eight distinct jokes, duelled pairwise against the judge's rubric
(Monday-specificity → real laugh → surprise → economy → freshness).

## The candidates

1. "I set five alarms for Monday. They unionized and demanded weekends off." (71 chars)
2. "My Monday alarm didn't ring — it sent a calendar invite: 'Disappointment, 7 AM.'" (80 chars) — **WINNER**
3. "Monday mornings should have a skip intro button. Netflix has one. My life doesn't." (82 chars)
4. "My weekend was a free trial. Monday is the subscription auto-renewing." (70 chars)
5. "Every Monday my bed files a restraining order against me. The judge keeps siding with the bed." (94 chars)
6. "The snooze button is Monday's way of letting you fail in nine-minute installments." (82 chars)
7. "I told Monday I'd be five minutes late. That was three coffees ago." (67 chars)
8. "Monday's motto is 'new week, new you.' Mine is 'same me, later.'" (64 chars)

## The duels

- **#2 vs #1** — #2 wins. Unionized alarms is a fun image, but alarm jokes are a crowded family; an alarm sending a calendar invite is the bigger, less predictable leap, and "Disappointment, 7 AM" is a sharper punchline.
- **#2 vs #3** — #2 wins. Skip-intro is relatable, but "My life doesn't" lands as an observation, not a punchline; #2 ends on an actual laugh.
- **#2 vs #4** — #2 wins. The free-trial metaphor is solid, but it explains itself and the punch is softer; #2's twist arrives faster and hits harder.
- **#2 vs #5** — #2 wins. The restraining-order image is funny, but the second sentence dilutes it; #2 is tighter and every word works.
- **#2 vs #6** — #2 wins, narrowly. The closest duel: #6 is the most economical and "nine-minute installments" is a genuinely original turn. But snooze-button jokes are a cliché family, while #2's twist (an alarm that sends a calendar invite) is more surprising and its punchline a bigger laugh — criteria 2 and 3 decide it.
- **#2 vs #7** — #2 wins. "Three coffees ago" is a quip, not a turn; less surprise, smaller laugh.
- **#2 vs #8** — #2 wins. "Same me, later" is a shrug, not a punchline; the mildest laugh of the eight.

## Winner rationale

#2 wins because it scores on every rubric angle in order: it is unmistakably about a Monday morning (alarm, 7 AM); it earns a real laugh with a concrete punchline ("Disappointment, 7 AM" as a calendar event title); its twist — the alarm doesn't fail, it escalates to a meeting invite — is not the first thing anyone would think of; at 80 characters it wastes no word; and the calendar-invite framing is fresh, not a rerun of the tired alarm/coffee Monday clichés.
